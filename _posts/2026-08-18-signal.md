## The Timeline

Based on the FL Studio project data, I started working on *Signal* all the way back on March 19th, 2024, when I was a (hardly sentient) 17-year-old. I then gradually lost interest in the song after composing myself into a corner; more on that later.

Not wanting to abandon the whole thing, a (slightly more sentient) 18-year-old me decided to cut his losses and release a way slower and shorter version of *Signal* in 2025, very creatively titled [*Lost Signal*]({% link _pages/youtube.md %}#lost-signal). Listening back on it, it was very obviously unfinished, but hey, better than nothing, right?

Well, I didn't stop there for some reason. From June to July 2026, due to personal circumstances (read: a horrible internship), a (reasonably sentient) 19-year-old me decided to lock in and actually *finish* the darn song. I completed it near the end of July, then spent a couple more weeks on the full music video.

So yeah, it roughly took 2.5 years, but honestly, the raw number of man-hours was probably only like 100 or so.

## The Specs

Some fun technical details ahead. I tried to keep the original structure relatively intact. I probably would've done things a lot differently had I restarted from scratch.

- All melodic tracks (i.e., main melody, backing melody, chords) use 3x Osc, because my past self liked hurting his ears apparently. Nevertheless, I tried to make them sound as gentle as possible.

- The drums are sampled from ``cw_amen14_175.wav`` from [Amen Breaks Volume 1](https://rhythm-lab.com/amen_breaks/). I love chopping drums.

- The "vocals" are sampled from [The Lincolnshire Poacher](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sXz6ghbfd2U) numbers station broadcast. Sort of the classic ARG-esque vibe going on here.

- The main melody is a musical realization of the VOX tones at the start of an SSTV transmission, see [here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EBEJu5r26sw) for an example. This is also where *Signal* gets its name from.

- The remaining sound effects (riser, reverse cymbal, cymbal crash, etc.) are either FL Studio presets or samples from [Cymatics](https://cymatics.fm/). That's good enough for me.

It's worth noting that, unlike all my previous attempts at it, this one is actually breakcore! It's 200 BPM and has a lot of rapid vocal/drum chops. That's basically breakcore. Please don't argue with me on this.

I still don't know how to mix or master and I refuse to learn it properly.

## The Sections

*Signal* is canonically divided into 8 sections: ``Inception``, ``Mechanism``, ``Reconnect``, ``Frequency``, ``Innocence``, ``Heartbeat``, ``Transcend``, and ``Exception``. The nice thing about having a name for each section is that I don't have to use timestamps.

Most of the names are self-explanatory, some of them less so. When you make a work of art, it's common for other people to find additional hidden meaning beyond your original intent. But as someone who makes a lot of... shit, I find myself noticing hidden connections in my own work all the time, and I end up subconsciously osmosing those interpretations. I don't know if there's a name for this phenomenon. Let's call it retroactive overfitting. Or retroactive projecting. I'm just projecting onto my own work.

Anyway, I have my reasons for why these names are "perfect", but that's just the retroactive projection speaking. I chose these names on a whim after finishing the track, and they've just stuck.

One thing I *can* say is that they're all 9 letters long. I did this on purpose so the music video would look "aesthetically pleasing". But then there are only 8 sections, so surely there must be a secret 9th one, right? This totally real [metapuzzle](https://www.puzzles.wiki/wiki/Spaghetti_(game)) is left as an exercise for the reader.

## The History

I don't exactly remember whether the main melody or backing melody came first, but I know I had the skeleton for the last part (``Exception``) very early on. Then ``Inception``, ``Mechanism``, and ``Frequency`` followed naturally. ``Innocence`` and ``Heartbeat`` came shortly after that too.

And then... nothing. I had no idea how to connect ``Heartbeat`` and ``Exception``. *Signal* went through many, many, *many* iterations. I considered dropping ``Exception`` entirely and making a new section instead, but in the end, most of the initial version remained in the final product.

One thing I find interesting is that the missing link (``Transcend``) wasn't a breakthrough at all. Despite its short length, it was a combination of several ideas I tried on and off over the months. A nice way to tie up all the loose ends, I think.

## The Inspirations

This list is not exhaustive and probably includes a lot of retroactive projecting. It's here so you can get a sense of how ``[adjective]`` my music taste is.

- The transition from ``Inception`` to ``Mechanism`` is partly based on Doin's [The Quick Brown Fox Jumps Over The Lazy Dog](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQsbtl5bapo), also around 200 BPM. If you squint at it, it also resembles µ-Ziq's [Hasty Boom Alert](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3c_E09gib-Y).

- The vocal chops in ``Frequency`` roughly follow the drum pattern in [Kyu-kurarin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2b1IexhKPz4) by Iyowa. I think I just had that earworm while I was making this section.

- For some reason, the slow melody progression in ``Innocence`` always plays in my head whenever I shower. Good ritual, I guess? Anyway, that progression predates this track, I had played around with it before.

- The transition from 200 BPM in ``Frequency`` to 100 BPM in ``Innocence`` is a reference to an old unreleased song I made, and no, you are not listening to it. The whole ``Innocence`` section is a reference to that song, now that I think about it.

- ``Heartbeat``'s build-up drum pattern is directly ripped off of Eptic's [Violence](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iD1wSeSQ1Dc).

- If you listen closely, you might notice that ``Transcend``'s vocal pattern follows the piano chops in [anybody can find love (except you.)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-NEGsRc3fbA) by hkmori.

- Last but not least, ``Exception`` and the music video itself are both references to [Credits](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EOTAWLaDa58) by Frums.

## The Video

I knew I wanted to do something special instead of just slapping [ZGameEditor Visualizer](https://www.image-line.com/fl-studio-learning/fl-studio-online-manual/html/plugins/ZGameEditor%20Visualizer.htm) on it and calling it a day. And because I hate using proper editing software more than I hate myself, I did the bulk of the work in Python.

First, the background image. I knew from the start that I wanted it to be grayscale with those long-exposure motion blur lines. But after trying (and failing) to take the photo near where I live, I decided to just set up a tripod in an unused classroom at my university (the academic year was starting soon) and capture myself walking across a white wall. I got kicked out by security shortly after, but let's not talk about that.

Next was the waveform, and for that I just used Aaron F. Bianchi's fine [LSaO Visualizer](https://aaron-f-bianchi.itch.io/lsao). "Extremely violent", it advertises. Wonderful. Overlaying it on top of the background image was straightforward enough.

Really, the only labor-intensive part was visualizing the lyrics. The numbers girl yaps so many digits in this song, and I wanted the timing of those digits to be as accurate as reasonably possible. I also had the freedom to control how long each chunk of digits was before moving on to the next one. By the end of the song, I chose to be a lot more liberal with the splitting and chunking, so you couldn't really follow along with the lyrics on the first watch. I think that enhances the overall chaotic vibe I was going for in those final sections.

I don't think there's much educational value in sharing the code, but [here](https://github.com/SanguineChameleon/signal) it is anyway. My mentality for these things has always been to just "get it working", so don't expect any easily readable (or runnable) code. A friendly reminder that no one cares about your code if you're the only one using it.

## Epilogue

And so we're at the end of this post.

You know, I find myself releasing creative works even when I'm unhappy with them more often than I'd like to admit. Of course, I get around to liking them after the initial sense of inadequacy, but you know, not ideal.

*Signal* has been in the works for over 2.5 years. Even after uploading the video unlisted on YouTube, I mulled it over for a few more days just to make sure I was ready and I didn't have any regrets before making it public.

But it really is out now, and I'm ready to leave it up on its own for good.

Is it perfect? No.

Am I happy with it? Yes.

Would I do something like this again? Probably not. Some things are just less enjoyable as an amateur than others. At least with drawing, I can visually see that I screwed up the anatomy or proportions or whatever, but with music production it's like... eh. Feels more like guesswork than anything. I am well aware the pros have their tips and tricks, but I kind of don't have the time and I kind of can't be bothered.

Nevertheless, this project deserves to be completed, despite me being a totally different person now than I was back when I started this.

So to those who still have works left unfinished, I hope they get to see the light one day.

The world can wait.
