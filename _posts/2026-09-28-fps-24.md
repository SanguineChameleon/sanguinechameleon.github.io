I followed the same guidelines I have for Project Euler: no genAI (of course), but I could refer to any online materials as long as they were available before the original contest date (November 2025).

I would like to thank [Yosupo Judge](https://judge.yosupo.jp/) for having so many formal power series problems, and the people who regularly use it to optimize their libraries. The stuff people do there is crazy; I mostly borrowed it wholesale whenever I needed to. That's why some of my submissions are in the tens of thousands of bytes.

Beyond the template bashing though, the problems were delightfully non-trivial, especially the last few. I don't think completing this problem set was the *hardest* thing I've ever done, but it's decently up there.

I haven't really looked at other people's write-ups (they're mostly in Japanese, naturally), but I get the feeling that some of my solutions were incredibly cursed and/or unintended by the problem authors. So I might write an unofficial editorial on the [Serviette](https://sanguinecha.me/serviette/) sometime soon, who knows. 

That's all from me. I am now slightly closer to knowing [how Elegia's mind works](https://codeforces.com/blog/Elegia).
